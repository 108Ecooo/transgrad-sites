Expertise

Freight in severe weather conditions

Snowfall on a mountain pass, black ice on the highway, a storm warning on a ferry line. You cannot cancel the weather — but you can plan for it.

The TRANSGRAD team · International logistics

Our routes run through regions with very different climates: the mountain passes of the South Caucasus, the winter highways of Mongolia and Kazakhstan, the summer heat of Iran and Central Asia. On every route, weather is not force majeure but a working factor that we account for at the planning stage.

Weather is built into the route in advance

Seasonality is known long before the trip: when the passes close, when the sandstorms begin, in which months heavy trucks are restricted. Every route has its own seasonal risk map, and delivery times are calculated with it in mind.

  • In winter, we build a time reserve into mountain segments and pre-calculate detours through lowland crossings.
  • In summer, for sensitive cargo on southern routes we plan around temperature peaks and select suitable rolling stock.
  • Before every trip we check the forecast along the entire route — not just at the origin and destination.

Monitoring en route

The plan is half the job. The other half is reacting to what happens in real time. We track road conditions and weather warnings for all active trips.

The best way to deal with a storm is not to get caught in it. A truck that waited out bad weather at a safe stop for a day usually arrives earlier than one that tried to «push through» a closed highway.

If a section of the road closes along the route, we decide quickly: wait it out, reroute or transfer the cargo to another mode of transport. The client learns about the change immediately — together with a new estimated deadline, not after the fact.

The cargo must be ready for the weather

Part of the weather risk is removed back at the shipper's warehouse. We agree packaging and securing requirements in advance, based on the conditions of the specific route.

  • Moisture-proof packaging and extra crating for trips through regions with swings in temperature and humidity.
  • Reinforced securing for mountain segments and unpaved roads.
  • Temperature-sensitive cargo travels only in rolling stock with the appropriate temperature regime.

The driver is the key link

No monitoring can replace an experienced driver who knows a specific pass in winter. We assign crews with experience on those exact routes to difficult destinations, and the decision to drive in dangerous conditions always rests with the driver: the safety of the crew and the cargo matters more than the schedule.

The result

Weather delays cannot be eliminated entirely — but they can be made rare, short and predictable. Our clients get an honest estimated deadline that accounts for the season, timely notifications when things change, and cargo that arrives intact in any weather.
