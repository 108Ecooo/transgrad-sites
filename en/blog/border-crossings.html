How we handle borders and customs

The border is the narrowest point of any international route. Here is how we turn it into a predictable stage instead of a lottery.

The TRANSGRAD team · International logistics

By our statistics, most missed deadlines in international freight happen not on the road, but at the border. A truck can cover three thousand kilometres on schedule and then lose a week at a crossing point — because of one error in the documents or an unfamiliar local regulation.

Over years of work on routes to Europe, the South Caucasus, Iran, Central Asia and China we have built a system that reduces border risks to a minimum. Here are the key principles.

Problem one: documents

The most frequent cause of downtime is discrepancies in documents: the weight in the CMR does not match the invoice, the HS code is imprecise, the certificate of origin is not in the form required by a specific crossing point.

Our rule: the complete document package is checked before the truck sets off — taking into account the requirements of every transit country, not just the destination. Each route has its own checklist, which we update whenever regulations change.

Problem two: queues and throughput

The queue at a busy crossing point can stretch to several days, and its length changes daily. We monitor the load on the crossings along our routes and factor it into the plan in advance.

  • We schedule arrival at the border in the least loaded «windows» — by day of the week and time of day.
  • We keep alternative crossing points in reserve and calculate rerouting in advance — not when the truck is already stuck.
  • We use advance electronic notification wherever it is available — it noticeably shortens clearance time.

Problem three: changing rules

On difficult routes regulations change fast: new labelling requirements appear, lists of goods subject to mandatory inspection change, seasonal restrictions on truck traffic are introduced.

The rule that applied last week may no longer work today. The only protection is fresh information from the border itself.

We get it from partners and brokers working directly at the crossing points. When something changes, we learn about it before our truck approaches the border — and have time to adjust the documents or the route.

Problem four: inspections

An inspection always costs time: unloading, recounting, reloading. It cannot be ruled out entirely, but its likelihood and cost can be reduced.

  • Accurate and complete declaration — cargo with transparent documents is inspected less often.
  • A smart loading scheme: if an inspection is ordered anyway, cargo stowed with possible unloading in mind saves hours.
  • A representative on site: our partners at the crossing supervise the procedure and keep it from dragging on.

What the client gets

For the client, all this work is invisible — and that is how it should be. They see only the result: the truck cleared the border on time, the cargo arrived on schedule. Border risks are our zone of responsibility, and we prefer to manage them rather than explain after the fact «why it turned out this way».
